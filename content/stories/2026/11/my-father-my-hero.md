My father was, and still is, my hero.

His name is **Kevin Lee Kennedy**. He’s my dad. He’s my Blue. And on November 16, he turns 60.

Dad is from Muncie, Indiana, and has called Columbus home for about the last 20 years. He retired from the United States Air Force after 21 years of service. For the first half of his military career, he served in military police. In the second half, he cross-trained into personnel.

He was so blue collar about the whole thing that he joked he was going to be a Walmart greeter when he got out. So they put “From Blue to Blue” on his plaque. I couldn’t make this up.

He didn’t become a greeter. He went to work at Honda in Greensburg, and today he’s one of the five longest-tenured people there. This guy is a tank.

My father has broken generational curses. Several. On his own back. I’m proud of his service, I’m proud of the man he is, and I could not be more proud to be his son.

> “I could never quite get a salute right, but you never gave me shit for it, Dad. Love you.”

## The words on my arm

When I was 18, I got my second tattoo ever. It says **“I want to make your sacrifice worthwhile.”** It’s a line from “Believe” by Yellowcard, my favorite band, a song they wrote as a thank you to the first responders of September 11.

I’m 34 now. Those words were always about him. This is my vow to make his sacrifice worth it.

## Why Back YOUR Blue

As we build Heart of Columbus, there is one part of it I want to share first: Back YOUR Blue.

The word YOUR matters. I want this to be about a person. Someone whose name you know, or whose kindness you remember even if you never caught their name. Someone who treated you with respect. Someone who showed up when you needed help.

For one person, that story might be about an officer who handled a difficult moment with dignity. For another, it might be an EMT, a firefighter, a dispatcher, or a veteran whose service deserves to be remembered.

This will be a gratitude section. A place to say thank you, to recognize a specific act of service, and to honor the person behind it. No political statement is required to tell someone they mattered to you.

For me, that person is my father. That’s where I’m starting. With Kevin Lee Kennedy.

Love you, Dad.

Jacob
