My first job was delivering a newspaper. I was 8 years old, and every morning I carried the Grand Forks Herald around Grand Forks Air Force Base in North Dakota. I kept that route until I was 12.

I got the idea from my dad. He was enlisted in the Air Force, and on top of that full time job he ran a rural paper route to make things work for our family. I watched him do it, and then I did it too.

A paper route teaches you one thing above everything else. Somebody is counting on you to show up, every day, before they wake up. Nobody thanks you when the paper is there, and everybody notices when it is not.

So here is a paper.

## Why print one

Heart of Columbus started on the web, and most of what we do will live there. But a newspaper you can hold does something a website cannot. It sits on the kitchen table. It gets passed across the counter at the diner. It gets clipped and stuck on the refrigerator. Somebody's grandmother reads it front to back.

This is our first try, a short run for friends and family. I want to hear what you think of it. There is a survey on the back page, and I mean it when I say tear it off and hand it to me.

## Why another paper at all

The Republic has covered Columbus since 1872, and I admire it. Anybody who puts out a newspaper that long has earned respect. I wish everyone who works there well.

But I believe a town this size deserves more than one voice, and I think a lot of people here are not hearing their own. That is my opinion, and I want to label it plainly as mine.

That is the promise of this paper. We keep news and opinion apart, and we label every piece so you always know which one you are reading. Every story names the person who wrote it and the person who checked it. Every story in this paper ends with a code you can scan with your phone to see the sources behind it. If we get something wrong, we fix it and say so, with the date.

## What you will find here

We will cover the businesses, churches, schools and teams that make this place what it is. We are bringing back faith coverage, which so many readers told me they missed. And we are starting Back YOUR Blue, a place to say thank you to the people who served. I start it in this issue with a story about my own father, and I hope you will send us yours.

Columbus has argued with itself in print since 1839. That is a healthy thing. We just want to make sure the heart of the town gets its say.

I have been making things work since I was 8. This is the next route, and I intend to show up for it.
